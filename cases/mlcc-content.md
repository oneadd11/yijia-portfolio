# Case 03: MLCC design engineering and reliability validation

## Overview

I developed material and structural designs for new MLCC products, evaluated their electrical stability and reliability, and used physical failure evidence to guide the next design build.

The work covered the full path from product targets to production readiness: design calculation, trial-build comparison, reliability qualification, failure analysis, design revision, burn-in screening, and release.

## 01 | Background

Each product began with predefined performance targets. My role was to translate those targets into feasible material and structural designs, then determine whether each design had enough electrical stability and structural robustness to progress toward production.

Meeting the capacitance target was only the first requirement. I also needed to understand how the material system and internal structure affected reliability risk before release.

## 02 | Design Window Development

I used design calculations and trial-build comparisons to narrow the design window across five variables:

- Ceramic powder options
- Dielectric layer thickness
- Layer count
- Internal electrode and structural configuration
- Manufacturing constraints

I did not select a design based on electrical compliance alone. I compared alternative combinations and balanced capacitance performance, structural margin, and manufacturability before choosing designs for further validation.

## 03 | Reliability Judgment

For each design build, I converted the product requirements and established internal criteria into measurable validation checkpoints.

| Validation input | What it measured | How I used the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TC / HALT | Response under accelerated stress | Exposed electrical or structural weakness |
| Capacitance / DF / IR | Electrical stability after stress | Measured drift against the expected performance window |
| Internal criteria | Acceptance limits | Determined whether the design could advance or required further review |

I reviewed post-stress behavior to determine whether a design remained within the expected performance window. Electrical drift or an abnormal response triggered additional design review, physical analysis, or re-validation. It was not treated as an isolated pass/fail result.

## 04 | Failure Analysis & Feedback

When abnormal units appeared, I independently performed DPA and SEM / EDS analysis to collect first-hand physical evidence.

Typical findings included:

- Ceramic cracking
- Internal electrode damage or melting

I compared each electrical signature with the observed failure morphology. This helped determine whether the weakness was more closely related to ceramic material selection, dielectric structure, layer configuration, or process variation.

I then returned the findings to the material and structural design review. The evidence identified which variables needed adjustment or another evaluation in the next trial build.

## 05 | Production Readiness

Updated designs went through electrical and reliability validation again before burn-in screening.

Burn-in was the final screening stage. It removed early-life weak units before qualified products moved to packaging and release.

## Results

| Result | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| 3 products | Progressed to mass production |
| 10+ models | Supported through design, validation, and qualification |

The work connected product requirements, design decisions, reliability evidence, failure interpretation, and re-validation in one engineering process. Each revision had to return to measurable evidence before the product could progress toward production.
